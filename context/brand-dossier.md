# PART 1 — THE BRAND

## 1.1 What the brand does — OBSERVED
SOLSKIN is a British direct-to-consumer self-tan brand. It sells a small, tight range of
sunless tanning products — mousse, gradual moisturiser, drops, an instant wash-off shimmer —
plus the application tools (mitt, brush, exfoliating gloves). Sold via its own Shopify store
and in **96 Boots stores across the UK and Ireland**.

Legal entity: **Solskin Group Limited**, company no. **14766564**, 9 Shottery Brook Office
Park, England, CV37 9NP (Stratford-upon-Avon). Contact: **hello@solskin.com**. Social presence
is **Instagram and TikTok only** — no Facebook, Pinterest, or YouTube linked anywhere.

## 1.2 The problem it solves — OBSERVED
Stated plainly on the About page: *"No more drying formulas, streaky fades, or tans that don't
get your skin."*

The founders' note names the problem precisely and personally: Kaci has **eczema** and could
never find a tan that didn't irritate or dry her skin; Grace has **acne-prone** skin and got
*"patchy fades, weird textures, and tans that just didn't work."* Their line: **"Self-tan
wasn't made for skin like ours. We were over it."**

So the problem is not "I want to be tanned." It's **"the entire self-tan category was
formulated for people whose skin doesn't react, and I'm not one of them."** That is a much
sharper problem than the category norm, and it's the whole brand.

## 1.4 Target audience & ideal customer

**OBSERVED — who the site actually shows and speaks to:**
- Women, roughly 18–35, UK.
- Segmented by **skin condition**: the PDP has a "SKIN TYPES WE HELP" module with creator
  testimonials tagged **ACNE**, **ACTIVE SKIN**, **ECZEMA**, **PSORIASIS**. The pop-up quiz
  segments by **Acne-Prone / Dry Skin / Oily Skin / Fine Lines**.
- Segmented by **skin tone**: "THE RIGHT GLOW FOR YOUR SKIN TONE" with creators tagged
  **LIGHT, FAIR, OLIVE, DEEP**.
- Heavily **creator-mediated**: 8+ named creators on the homepage grid (4k → 1.5m followers),
  more on the PDP. Founder Kaci Jay is herself a 1.1m-follower creator.
- Price-and-offer sensitive: Klarna, bundle discounts up to 29%, free-gift hooks, a points
  programme, a Louis Vuitton giveaway.

**INFERRED — the ideal customer:**
A young British woman who wants to look sun-kissed year-round and has a specific reason to
distrust self-tan: her skin reacts, or she's had visibly bad results. She has probably tried
Bondi Sands or a supermarket tan and abandoned the bottle. She discovers brands through TikTok
and Instagram, trusts creators far more than brands, checks reviews before buying, and buys on
a trigger — a holiday, a birthday, a night out, the first warm weekend.

She is not looking to be "fixed." The About page is explicit about this: *"This isn't about
fixing anything, it's about making us feel good on our skin."* That is a meaningful posture
choice in a category that routinely sells insecurity.

## 1.5 Market & category — OBSERVED / INFERRED
Category: UK sunless tanning / body care, mid-market, £15–£22 hero price.
Competitive set (INFERRED — **not named on the site**): Bondi Sands, St. Tropez, Tan-Luxe,
Isle of Paradise, Coco & Eve, b.tan, Skinny Tan.
The site's own comparison table doesn't name anyone — it just says **"Others."**

**INFERRED positioning within the category:** SOLSKIN is the *skincare-credible* option.
Bondi Sands owns volume and price. St. Tropez owns heritage. Isle of Paradise owns
colour-correction. Tan-Luxe owns premium drops. SOLSKIN's available lane is **skin
sensitivity** — and it's the only one the incumbents can't easily claim without reformulating.

## 1.6 Value proposition — OBSERVED
Stated most cleanly on the PDP: *"Your fast-pass to a deep bronzed hydrated glow with no
streaks, no orange, and no drama. Made with ingredients that love your skin."*

And in the seven-row comparison table (SOLSKIN vs Others):
scientifically formulated for sensitive skin · looks natural, never fake · hydrates like
skincare · dermatologist-approved · customisable, even & buildable glow · no biscuit smell ·
transfer-free.

**INFERRED — the proposition in one sentence:** *A tan that behaves like skincare, so people
whose skin reacts to everything else can finally have one.*

## 1.7 Positioning statement — OBSERVED (as used on site)
> **"UK'S NO1 RATED TAN FOR SENSITIVE SKIN"**

Deployed in the rotating announcement bar and as the homepage H2 above the product carousel.

## 1.8 Differentiation — what actually sets it apart
**OBSERVED, in descending order of defensibility:**
1. **Founder truth.** Two founders with the exact conditions the product addresses (eczema,
   acne-prone). Not a positioning exercise — a reason the company exists.
2. **Sensitive skin as the organising principle**, not a footnote. It's in the tagline, the
   comparison table, the PDP module structure, and the pop-up quiz.
3. **Boots distribution** — 96 doors. Independent third-party validation a pure DTC can't buy.
4. **A 1.1m-follower founder-creator.** Kaci Jay is both the founder and the brand's largest
   media channel. Very few competitors have this.
5. **"No skin retouching or filters applied."** A stated honesty policy on before/after
   imagery, in a category built on filters.
6. **Real skin in the imagery.** The before/after shots show visible spots, marks and texture,
   unretouched.
7. **Made in Britain**, vegan, cruelty-free, dermatologically tested.

**INFERRED — the most ownable of these:** #1, #5 and #6 together. Formulation claims are
copyable; a founder's eczema and a no-retouching policy are not.

## 1.9 Mission & belief — OBSERVED
> *"We're building a community that celebrates real skin: dry, sensitive, acne-prone,
> textured, scarred, freckled, melanin-rich, melanin-fair, and everything in between. Because
> tanning isn't about hiding, it's about glowing your way."*

> *"We created SOLSKIN to love your skin back... because everyone deserves to glow without
> compromising their skin. This isn't about fixing anything."* — Kaci & Grace

**INFERRED — the belief underneath:** tanning has historically been sold as correction. SOLSKIN
argues it should be expression. "Glowing your way" is the thesis; the inclusive skin list is
the proof they mean it.

## 1.10 Brand promise & essence
- **Promise (OBSERVED, paraphrased):** you will get a real, natural, buildable glow, and your
  skin will be better for it — not punished by it.
- **Essence (INFERRED):** **kind confidence.** Bold enough to shout in condensed caps, kind
  enough to have been built by people whose skin was let down.
- **How it wants to be perceived (INFERRED):** the tan your friend with sensitive skin
  swears by — modern, credible, warm, a little bit cool, not clinical, not try-hard.

# PART 4 — TONE OF VOICE

## 4.1 Description a copywriter can work from
SOLSKIN speaks like **a well-informed friend who has been let down by this category herself.**
She's warm, direct and a bit funny. She uses "you" and "we", never "customers." She leads with
how something feels, then backs it with a fact, and she never lectures. She's confident
without being boastful — the confidence comes from the product working, not from adjectives.
She swears by specifics ("6–8 hours", "£0.95 per tan", "97.2%") rather than superlatives.

## 4.2 Mechanics — OBSERVED
- **Formality:** low. Contractions everywhere. Fragments used deliberately.
- **Person:** second person dominant; first-person plural for the brand; first-person singular
  in the founders' quotes.
- **Sentence style:** short. Often verbless. Frequently a list of three negatives — *"no
  streaks, no orange, and no drama."* This triple-negative rhythm is a recognisable tic and
  worth keeping.
- **Vocabulary:** everyday, with a light Gen-Z inflection ("vibes", "Qs", "hook you up",
  "fast-pass", "we've got you", "nasties"). British spelling throughout (moisturiser, colour).
- **Emotional register:** reassuring, celebratory, never anxious or shaming.
- **Technical level:** low by default, with specifics available on demand. Ingredients are
  always translated into benefit: *"Aloe Vera = Soothes + nourishes."*
- **Emoji:** used, sparingly, at the end of a sentence — 💛 🫶 ✨. Never mid-sentence.
- **Headline style:** ALL CAPS, short, declarative, often a question-and-answer in one
  ("THIS FORMULA? IT'S TRIED, TESTED & THAT GOOD").

## 4.3 A consistency problem — OBSERVED
The voice is noticeably **younger and looser on the quiz page** (*"Ready for a glow-up that's
100% you?"*, *"the glow that vibes with your skin tone and style"*) than on the PDP, which is
more measured and benefit-led. The About page is warmer and more emotional than either. These
read as three different writers.

**RECOMMENDATION:** keep all three registers but assign them deliberately — *warm/emotional*
for brand and story pages, *clear/benefit-led* for product and conversion, *playful/Gen-Z* for
quiz, community, rewards and social. Write that mapping down so it survives new hires.

# PART 5 — IMAGERY & CREATIVE DIRECTION

## 5.1 The four photography modes — OBSERVED
1. **Sun-drenched lifestyle (hero).** Autoplay muted looping video: a whitewashed Mediterranean
   villa, terracotta beams, strong midday sun, a model in a brown bikini and crochet hat. Warm,
   golden, holiday, expensive-feeling. Reads Ibiza — consistent with the "Kaci-Jay's Ibiza Edit"
   bundle name.
2. **Clean studio product.** Bottles on plain cream/white, soft even light, no props. The
   packaging is sand-coloured with a dark brown wordmark rotated 90° up the bottle. Restrained,
   skincare-aisle, deliberately *not* the neon of the mass tan brands.
3. **Real-skin proof.** Before/after pairs on plain backgrounds, model in white underwear,
   **visible spots, marks and texture left in**, captioned *"No skin retouching or filters
   applied."* A redhead with freckles and fair skin features prominently. This is the most
   distinctive and most valuable imagery the brand has.
4. **Creator UGC.** Vertical 1080p phone video, click-to-play with sound, framed with the
   creator's @handle, follower count and a role label (CUSTOMER / SKIN EXPERT / INFLUENCER) —
   plus, on the PDP, a skin-condition or skin-tone label.

## 5.2 Set-design & still life — OBSERVED
Bundle imagery is genuinely charming and more art-directed than the rest of the site: the
Birthday Edit is styled with a cream frosted cake, cherries, a lit candle and gold confetti,
with "*The* BIRTHDAY edit" set in a script face over it. Warm, tactile, giftable.

## 5.3 Graphics & decorative elements — OBSERVED
Thin-line custom SVG benefit icons; pill-shaped label chips; a large wordmark used as a footer
graphic; script-face accents (Caveat) on lifestyle graphics; award roundel; press logos in
white on dusty-rose cards.

## 5.4 What the imagery is doing — INFERRED
Two jobs, held in tension and mostly resolved well:
- **Aspiration** (hero video, Ibiza, golden light) — makes you want the glow.
- **Honesty** (before/after, real texture, no retouching, diverse tones and conditions) —
  makes you believe the glow is achievable on *your* skin.

The aspiration imagery is doing the selling; the honesty imagery is doing the converting. The
honesty work is rarer, more ownable, and currently buried below the fold.

## 5.5 What is missing — OBSERVED
- **No founder photography of Kaci and Grace anywhere on the site**, despite the founder story
  being the strongest differentiator. The About page has a heading *"MESSAGE FROM OUR FOUNDER
  KACI JAY"* — the media behind it did not render for me, so I cannot confirm what it contains.
- No manufacturing, lab, formulation or dermatologist imagery — the "science-backed" and
  "made in Britain" claims have no visual evidence.
- No Boots / in-store photography.

The architecture is sound. The problem is that step one currently sells a handbag.

# PART 7 — CONTENT STRATEGY

## 7.1 What it educates on — OBSERVED
Ingredients and their benefits · how to apply (four clear steps: prep, apply, wait 6–8 hours,
maintain) · shade selection by tone · which product suits which skin condition · delivery and
returns · cost per application.

## 7.2 How it builds authority — OBSERVED
Not through owned expertise but through **borrowed and aggregated credibility**: dermatologist
approval (unnamed), press quotes, retail listing, awards, review volume, and above all
creators. There is no blog, no journal, no education hub, no named expert, no published study.

## 7.3 How it handles objections — OBSERVED
| Objection | Where it's answered |
|---|---|
| "It'll irritate my skin" | Tagline, comparison table, FAQ, ingredients, creator testimonials by condition |
| "I'll go orange / it'll look fake" | "Looks natural, never fake", before/afters, shade visualiser |
| "It'll be streaky" | "Streak free" benefit icon, mitt/brush upsells, how-to-apply |
| "It'll smell" | "No biscuit smell" comparison row |

| "What if it doesn't work?" | 30-day money-back guarantee |

This is a thorough objection map — better than most DTC sites.

## 7.4 What it leaves out — OBSERVED

- **No sustainability or packaging content** at all.
- **No named dermatologist** — "dermatologist-approved" and "our in-house experts" are never

- No editorial content of any kind — no blog, guides, or seasonal content. All SEO value has

# PART 8 — PAGE BY PAGE

### Homepage — `/`
**Purpose:** capture, establish credibility, route to product. **Audience:** cold and returning
traffic. **Main message:** *currently* — win a Louis Vuitton bag. *Intended* — the UK's No.1
rated tan for sensitive skin. **Role:** the whole funnel in one scroll. **What it says about
the brand:** popular, social, promotional, warm — but currently more "there's a deal on" than
"we solved a problem for people like you."

### About us — `/pages/about-us`
**Purpose:** the emotional and credibility core. **Main message:** two founders with eczema and
acne-prone skin built the tan the category never made for them. **Key content:** Our Story,
six benefit icons, "A NOTE FROM OUR FOUNDERS", "REAL SKIN, REAL GLOW", a founder message
module, product recommendations. **CTA:** "Shop Your Perfect Tan." **Role:** converts the
sceptic — the visitor who needs a *reason* to trust before a *rating*. **INFERRED:** this is
the best-written page on the site and one of the least trafficked. Its content should be
pulled forward into the homepage.

# PART 9 — WHAT MAKES THIS BRAND UNIQUE

## 9.1 Distinctive and ownable — preserve at all costs
1. **Vanguard CF in all caps.** The single most recognisable visual element. Bold, condensed,
   poster-like. Without it the site would be generic.
2. **The self-tan palette.** Cream → sand → brown → espresso. The brand is literally coloured

# PART 10 — WHAT SHOULD IMPROVE
*Reasoning given for each. These are opportunities, not complaints.*

## 10.1 The hero sells the wrong thing — highest priority
**Observed:** the largest, most valuable space on the site is a Louis Vuitton giveaway with
the CTA "BUY NOW TO WIN". The actual positioning line is demoted to a rotating announcement
bar and a mid-page H2.
**Why it matters:** a cold visitor's first impression is "sweepstake", not "the tan for
sensitive skin." Giveaway traffic converts worse and retains worse than proposition traffic,
and the promotion is temporary while the positioning is permanent.
**Recommendation:** the sensitive-skin proposition owns the hero permanently. Promotions run in
the announcement bar and in a secondary slot.

## 10.2 Four simultaneous interstitials, and a scroll lock
**Observed:** on load, a full-screen Klaviyo modal and a rewards overlay appear together; the
modal sets `body { overflow: hidden }`, so the page cannot be scrolled until it is dismissed.
Behind them sit a sticky "CLAIM FREE MITT" tab and a floating "WIN A LOUIS VUITTON BAG" pill.
**Why it matters:** two stacked modals is a dark-pattern impression, and a hard scroll lock on
mobile is the most reliable bounce generator there is. Two free-gift offers competing with each
other also devalues both.
**Recommendation:** one entry offer, delayed or exit-intent, never scroll-locking. Retire the
duplicate.

## 10.3 Floating promos obscure content and CTAs
**Observed on mobile:** the "WIN A LOUIS VUITTON BAG" pill sits over the "Shop All" button and
over product imagery; the "CLAIM FREE MITT" tab overlays product cards and the comparison
table. The hero H1 is **clipped off the right edge** ("JOIN SOL SOCIETY REWARDS TO |") and the
subhead is truncated mid-sentence ("...Louis Vuitton bag. T..."). The trust strip is also cut.
**Why it matters:** these are straightforward mobile layout bugs on the highest-traffic
viewport, and one of them literally covers a purchase CTA.
**Recommendation:** fix as bugs regardless of the redesign; enforce a rule that no floating
element may overlap a CTA or product image.

## 10.4 The founder story is buried
**Observed:** the strongest differentiator — two founders with the exact skin conditions the
product addresses — appears only on the About page, with no photography of them anywhere.
**Why it matters:** formulation claims are copyable; a founder's eczema is not. It is also the
brand's best answer to "why should I believe you?"
**Recommendation:** bring Kaci and Grace onto the homepage with real photography and the
"Self-tan wasn't made for skin like ours" line. Given Kaci's 1.1m following, her face is also
a performance asset.

## 10.5 Unsubstantiated claims — a regulatory exposure
**Observed:** "UK'S NO1 RATED TAN FOR SENSITIVE SKIN" with no stated source; "97.2% say they'd
never go back" with no source or date; "dermatologist-approved" with no named dermatologist;
"250k+ customers" undated. Separately, the PDP states aloe *"Calms irritation (eczema,
psoriasis)"* and the drops copy claims *"Zero Skin Irritation & Inflammation"*, while creator
modules are tagged ECZEMA and PSORIASIS.
**Why it matters:** UK cosmetics advertising is ASA/CAP-regulated. Absolute claims ("zero
irritation") and claims that read as treating a named medical condition are the two things
most likely to draw a ruling — and SOLSKIN's Boots listing raises the stakes.
**Recommendation:** hold substantiation for every number; name the derm testing house or a
named dermatologist; soften "Zero Skin Irritation" to formulation language; keep the
condition-tagged testimonials but frame them as individual customer experience.
*This is a flag, not a diagnosis — a UK cosmetics claims specialist should review.*

## 10.6 Catalogue bloat and naming inconsistency
**Observed:** 30+ live products, many functionally identical with different names — "Glow
Starter Kit - Mousse" and "Tan Mousse & Drops" are the same SKU (`SOL_MD200_D25`) at the same
price (£38.69). The same shade variant `SOL_GLM200` is labelled "LIGHT TO MEDIUM" in one
bundle and "FAIR TO MEDIUM" in another. Expired seasonal bundles (Christmas, Halloween
"Witch, please") are still live. The Gradual Tanning Moisturiser's URL handle is
`gradual-tanning-moisturiser-bundle` though it is not a bundle, and its product description is
**empty**. The Sculpt & Glow Brush carries the **Tan Mousse's description** copy-pasted.
**Why it matters:** duplicate SKUs split reviews and confuse choice; inconsistent shade names
undermine the shade-selection journey the site is otherwise good at; empty and wrong
descriptions cost SEO and trust.
**Recommendation:** rationalise to one canonical name per bundle, one shade vocabulary
(pick "Light to Medium"), archive expired seasonals, fix handles, write real copy per SKU.

## 10.7 Design-token drift
**Observed:** two browns (`#885946` / `#805B4A`), two creams (`#F6F0EB` / `#F4F0EB`), seven
font families loaded, and a comparison table using red/green outside the palette.
**Why it matters:** it's invisible individually and cumulatively makes the site feel less
crafted than the packaging does.
**Recommendation:** one token file. Theme third-party widgets. Drop Bebas Neue, Open Sans,
Poppins. Redraw the comparison ticks in brand brown and sand.

## 10.8 Carousel-heavy information architecture
**Observed:** five or more horizontal carousels on the homepage; several product cards are
partially hidden off the right edge on mobile.
**Why it matters:** carousels hide content — engagement drops sharply after the first slide,
and the brand's best proof (creators, press, comparison) is inside them.
**Recommendation:** convert the highest-value proof (comparison table, stats, founder story)
to static, full-width layouts. Keep carousels for genuinely browsable sets.

## 10.9 Discovery under-served
**Observed:** the shade quiz is a mega-menu item and a pop-up; there is no route from the
homepage hero into "find your shade."
**Why it matters:** "which one do I need and will it match my tone?" is the primary blocker
for a first-time self-tanner, and the quiz is the brand's best answer to it.
**Recommendation:** make the quiz a primary homepage CTA alongside Shop.

## 10.10 Retail and subscription both hidden
**Observed:** 96 Boots stores appears only as a footer link; the 10%-off subscription exists in
the product data but is not visible on the PDP.
**Why it matters:** Boots is third-party validation a DTC brand can't manufacture, and
subscription is the cheapest LTV lever available for a consumable bought every few weeks.
**Recommendation:** an "As stocked in Boots" trust element near the hero; surface subscribe-
and-save as a visible option in the PDP buy box.

## 10.11 No editorial layer
**Observed:** no blog, guides, or education content of any kind.
**Why it matters:** every long-tail search around sensitive skin and self-tan — exactly where
this brand should be unbeatable — is currently ceded to competitors and magazines.
**Recommendation:** a small, high-quality guide hub owning "self tan for sensitive skin",
"self tan and eczema", "how to stop self tan going patchy", "self tan for fair/freckled skin".

# PART 11 — FINAL BRAND UNDERSTANDING (THE DNA BRIEF)

**Who they are.** SOLSKIN — a British self-tan brand founded by Kaci and Grace, two women whose
own skin (eczema; acne-prone) was failed by every tan they tried. Solskin Group Limited,
Stratford-upon-Avon. Sold direct and in 96 Boots stores.

**What they do.** Make sunless tanning products — mousse, gradual moisturiser, drops, shimmer —
formulated so that people with reactive skin can have a glow without paying for it in
irritation. Vegan, cruelty-free, dermatologically tested, made in Britain.

**Who they serve.** Young British women who love a tan and have a specific reason to distrust
it: sensitive, eczema-prone, acne-prone, dry or reactive skin, or a history of streaky,
orange, smelly results. She discovers through TikTok and Instagram, trusts creators, reads
reviews, and buys against an occasion.

**Why they matter.** They reframed a category built on correction into one about expression,
and they did it from lived experience rather than a positioning workshop. *"This isn't about
fixing anything."*

**How they position.** The UK's No.1 rated tan for sensitive skin. Skincare credibility inside
a tanning product.

**What they believe.** That everyone deserves to glow without compromising their skin, that
real skin — textured, scarred, freckled, melanin-rich, melanin-fair — is the point rather than
the problem, and that tanning is about glowing your way, not hiding.

**How they communicate.** Like a warm, funny, well-informed friend. Second person, short
sentences, contractions, British spelling, light Gen-Z inflection, occasional emoji. Feeling
first, fact second. Specific numbers over superlatives. Triple negatives —
*"no streaks, no orange, no drama."*

**How they look.** Heavy condensed all-caps display type (Vanguard CF) over a four-tone warm
neutral palette drawn from the product itself — cream, sand, brown, espresso. DM Sans for
everything else. Pill buttons, softly rounded cards, almost no shadow, no sharp corners. Full-
bleed colour bands. Sun-drenched Mediterranean lifestyle imagery alongside clean studio product
and honest, unretouched before/afters.

**How they make customers feel.** Relieved (finally, one that won't react), confident (I look
sun-kissed, not fake), and included (my skin is in the picture).

**What makes them different.** A founder story that is the product story. A no-retouching
policy in a filtered category. Proof segmented by skin condition *and* skin tone. Boots
distribution. A 1.1m-follower founder.

**What they own visually.** Vanguard CF caps. The tan-shade palette. The vertical wordmark on
sand packaging. Pill geometry. The SOL— naming system. "Glow."

**What must be preserved in a redesign.** The display type and its all-caps treatment. The
four warm neutrals — resolved to single values. Pill and soft-corner geometry. The voice.
Real, unretouched skin and the no-filter statement. Proof by condition and by tone. The
inclusive skin litany. The SOL— family. "Glow" as the central word.

**What should evolve.** Give the hero back to the proposition and put the founders in it. One
entry offer, never a scroll lock, no floating element over a CTA. Bring Boots, subscription and
the quiz up out of the footer and the menu. Rationalise the catalogue and the shade vocabulary.
Collapse to one design-token file and three fonts. Substantiate or soften the claims. Turn the
best proof out of carousels into static, confident layout. Add a small editorial layer that
makes SOLSKIN the definitive answer for self-tan on sensitive skin.

**The one-line brief for whoever rebuilds this:**
> *Same brand, more conviction. SOLSKIN already knows exactly who it is — two women who
> couldn't find a tan their skin could tolerate, so they made one. Build a site that leads with
> that instead of a handbag giveaway, keep the big condensed caps and the tan-coloured palette,
> keep the real unretouched skin, and get out of the visitor's way.*
