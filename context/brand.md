## Claims we must NOT make — and two the site currently does
Standing rules: no medical or treatment claims; no "hypoallergenic"; no "clinically proven"
without a citable study; **never imply sun protection**; never imply tanned skin is better.

**Two live exposures found on the site — flag to George, don't repeat in new work:**
1. Tan Drops copy claims **"Zero Skin Irritation & Inflammation"** — an absolute claim.
2. PDP ingredient copy states aloe *"Calms irritation (eczema, psoriasis)"*, and creator
   modules are tagged ECZEMA / PSORIASIS — this reads as treating named medical conditions
   with a cosmetic.
UK cosmetics ads are ASA/CAP-regulated and the Boots listing raises the stakes. A UK claims
specialist should review. Until then, use formulation language ("formulated for sensitive
skin", "soothing ingredients") and frame condition testimonials as individual experience.

## Creative guardrail: where product meets body — [George flagged this 2026-08-29]
**Never show product liquid landing on, running down, or pooling on a torso, chest or
cleavage** — in 3D, film, stills or ads. Applying tan to a limb is the honest, neutral image
and it's what people actually do (you patch-test on your inner arm, you apply to arms and
legs with a mitt).

**Why:** a droplet landing on a woman's chest with a glow blooming from the impact point reads
sexually whatever the intent, and it argues directly against the thing SOLSKIN is for —
*"this isn't about fixing anything, it's about making us feel good on our skin"*, founded by
two women, sold to women, built on self-care and real bodies. It's not merely risky, it's
off-brand.

**How to apply:** land product on forearms, hands, shoulders, lower legs. Keep the camera wide
enough that a body reads as a person in context, not a cropped part. Labelled before/after
photography of a full figure is fine — that's documentary proof and the brand publishes it.
The line is product-on-body as a *sensual event*.

## Brand tensions to hold
Confident, never cocky · science-backed, never clinical · playful, never juvenile ·
**loud typography, quiet palette** (this is the signature tension — keep it) ·
beauty-brand energy with skincare-brand credibility.
